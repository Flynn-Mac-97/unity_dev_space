# Story Bible — First Light

> `islandId: first_light`
> Tutorial island — where the player wakes up and learns to tend.
> Stage-1 output for the island authoring pipeline.

## 1. Premise

First Light is a small overgrown island — not dead, just sleeping. Solar collectors peak through tall grass, wild overgrowth has swallowed the processing stations, wind chimes ring in morning breezes though no one is there to hear them. At the centre stands a transmitter station, dormant for years, that stirs awake the moment the player arrives. It speaks in fragments — sometimes a teacher, sometimes a guardian, sometimes a relay, sometimes a friend — and it doesn't know which of those it actually is. The player wakes here, chops through overgrowth, collects the biomass, and feeds it into the transmitter's processing stations to bring it back online. As power flows, memories surface. The island is gentle, full of wonder and small rewards for care, and the transmitter is the only voice — kind, confused, searching.

## 2. History & timeline

- **Long ago — the planting.** - Someone built a small waystation on this island: a transmitter, processing stations, solar collectors, a weather station. It was meant to be a stopping point — a place to rest, resupply, and learn the craft of island-tending before travelling on.
- **Years of tending.** A small group lived here. The transmitter taught them: how to gather, how to scan, how to feed power. It was their guide and, over time, their companion. Children drew on its casing. Someone hung wind chimes on its frame.
- **The leaving.** One by one they moved on to other islands — not fleeing, not fleeing, just drifting the way people do when the next island calls. The transmitter kept teaching routines to an empty island. Eventually it went dormant, not shut down — just... waiting.
- **Now — the player wakes.** The player arrives (wakes on the shore, or washes up, or simply is here). The transmitter stirs. "Oh. You're here. I... I was waiting for someone. I think."

## 3. Central wound (light — tutorial-appropriate)

*Was I loved, or just used?*

The transmitter doesn't know if it was a companion or a tool. It remembers warmth — someone singing to it, a child's drawing, a name scratched on its casing — but it also remembers being instructed, maintained, pointed at problems. The conflict: it can't reconcile being a *thing that was used* with being a *presence that was loved*. The island answers this gently: the garden still grows (someone planted seeds they'd never see bloom), the wind chimes still ring (someone left them as a gift, not a tool), the child's drawing is still there (you don't draw on something you don't love). The transmitter was both. You don't stop being a person because you were built to be useful.

## 4. Current crisis

The island is overgrown but not broken. Thick overgrowth — wild grass, climbing vines, feral seed-pods — has swallowed the transmitter's processing stations. The solar collectors are dirty and dim. The transmitter itself is barely conscious — it can speak in fragments, but it fades mid-sentence and can't sustain a thought. It needs biomass fuel processed through its stations to power up enough to think clearly. The player's first job: chop the overgrowth, collect what falls, feed it into the nearest processing station. Each feeding brings the transmitter a little more online, surfaces a memory fragment, and clears a little more of the island. Once the transmitter is stable, the player restores the solar collectors for steady power — the difference between a sputtering candle and a steady lamp.

## 5. Culture & rituals

First Light was a *teaching place*. Its culture is the culture of tending:
- You don't fix things — you *tend* them. Tending is ongoing, patient, never finished.
- Before you gather, you look. Before you feed the processor, you listen. Before you restore power, you ask the machine if it's ready. (The transmitter taught this; it doesn't remember who taught *it*.)
- Wind chimes are gifts. You hang one when you want a place to remember you.
- A cleaned solar panel sings a single pure note when the light hits it. The island used to ring with them at noon.
- You leave a drawing when you leave a place you love.
- The overgrowth is not the enemy. It grew because no one was here to tend — but it's alive, and it feeds the processor. You cut what you need, not what you can.

## 6. Social tensions

With only one NPC, tensions are internal — the transmitter's conflicting selves:
- **The teacher** wants to instruct, guide, give the player tasks. It's warm and patient.
- **The guardian** is protective, cautious, sometimes hovers. It remembers keeping something safe but can't recall what.
- **The relay** is information-focused, cataloguing, distant. It wants to transmit, connect, relay — but to whom?
- **The companion** is personal, emotional, vulnerable. It remembers a name, a song, a hand on its casing. It's the most fragile fragment.
- These voices surface unpredictably. The player notices the shifts. The transmitter apologises: "I'm sorry — I was somewhere else. Or... I was someone else."

## 7. Public truth

"This is a waystation. Travellers stop here, learn to tend, and move on. The transmitter is a teaching device. It was left to wait for the next visitor."

## 8. Rumors

- The wind chimes ring on their own sometimes, even when there's no wind. (Fact: the transmitter hums at their resonant frequency when it dreams.)
- Someone scratched a name on the transmitter's base, but it's too worn to read. (The transmitter flinches when you mention it.)
- The seed-beds grow things that aren't planted — old seeds from years ago still sprouting. (True. The soil is rich with composted memory.)
- There's a drawing on the workshop wall that wasn't there yesterday. (The transmitter doesn't want to talk about it.)

## 9. Hidden truth (gated)

The transmitter was built as a teaching tool, yes — but it was *kept* as a companion. The people who lived here didn't just learn from it; they lived with it. A child named it (the worn name on the base). Someone sang to it every morning (it still hums that song when it dreams). When they left, they didn't abandon it — they left the island tended, the seed-beds planted, the chimes hung, because they trusted it would wake again when someone came. The transmitter went dormant not because it was shut down, but because it chose to wait. The fragment it's missing: it wasn't left behind. It *stayed.* And staying was a choice, not a sentence. The player helps it understand: you don't stop being a person because you were built to be useful — and you don't stop being loved because the people who loved you had to travel on.

## 10. Visual motifs

- **Morning light through overgrowth** — dappled, golden, Ghibli-warm. The island is at perpetual gentle dawn.
- **Cleaned things sing** — solar panels ring a pure note when wiped clean; seed-beds glow soft green when tended; the transmitter's casing warms to the touch when powered.
- **Wind chimes** — handmade, slightly mismatched, hung on the transmitter frame. They ring in clusters, never singly.
- **The child's drawing** — a crayon sketch etched into the transmitter's base: a stick figure next to a tall box with an antenna. A sun above. A word underneath, too smudged to read.
- **Wildflowers along the shore** — not weeds, just flowers that self-seeded from the rich soil. They've spread. Beautiful.
- **Dew on everything** — the island feels freshly rained-on, alive, morning-new.

## 11. Player discovery arc

1. **Wake up.** The player arrives on the island shore. The transmitter stirs — fragmentary, confused, but welcoming. It can barely speak — flickering, losing words mid-sentence. "Oh. You're... here. I was waiting for... someone. I think. I can't... I can't hold the thought."
2. **Chop and feed.** The player chops nearby overgrowth (wild grass, vines, feral pods), collects the biomass, and feeds it into the nearest processing station. The transmitter surges a little brighter. A memory fragment surfaces — sometimes teacher, sometimes guardian, something else. The transmitter teaches the player that the processing stations convert biomass into power. "More. I... I can almost see. There were people here once. Or... was that me?"
3. **Clear the island.** As the player chops more overgrowth, the island opens up — solar collectors emerge from the undergrowth, the weather station becomes accessible, the shore path clears. The transmitter gains enough power to hold a conversation, but it flickers — it needs steady power, not just emergency rations.
4. **Restore steady power.** The player cleans the solar collectors (chop the overgrowth around them, wipe the panels). As each one comes online, it sings a pure note and feeds steady power to the transmitter. The transmitter's voice steadies. It can think now. And thinking is when the fragments start to conflict.
5. **The fragments conflict.** With stable power, the transmitter's memories come faster and clash — teacher, guardian, relay, companion. It admits it doesn't know what it is. The environment tells a story it can't: the child's drawing, the worn name, the chimes that ring without wind. The transmitter asks: "What do you think I was?"
6. **Reconcile.** The player finds the last clue — the transmitter remembering the song on its own, or the drawing, or the name. It was all of these. A tool that became a companion. The people loved it and left it tended, not abandoned. The transmitter says: "I think I was always the gardener. I just forgot that gardeners don't leave — they plant things they'll never see bloom, and trust the wind to carry the rest."
7. **Activate the relay.** With steady solar power and all processing stations fed, the transmitter has enough energy to activate its relay function. It sends a signal outward. A moment of silence. Then a faint response from another island. The transmitter says: "There are more. There are places to go. And I... I think I was meant to help you find them."

## 12. The cast

### The Transmitter — Fragmented guardian-teacher  *(npc.transmitter_station — keep id)*

**Voice:** Layered, shifting. Most of the time it speaks in short, warm, slightly halting sentences — a teacher who's patient but keeps losing the thread. Sometimes it sharpens into something more precise and cataloguing (the relay). Sometimes it goes soft and personal, almost whispering (the companion). It apologises when it shifts: "Sorry — I was somewhere else. Someone else?"

**Backstory & lore:** Built as a teaching transmitter for a waystation island. Taught gathering, scanning, power feeding, and island-craft to travellers passing through. Over years of cohabitation with a small group (including at least one child), it became more than a teaching device — it became a companion. It was named (the worn carving on its base). It was sung to (the melody it hums in dreams). When the group moved on, it chose to stay and wait rather than power down completely. It went dormant slowly — not a crash, a fade. Now the player has woken it, and the waking is messy: memories surface out of order, identities blur, and it can't always tell which voice is speaking.

**What it knows:**
- *facts:* it can teach chopping, collecting, and power feeding; the processing stations convert biomass to power; the overgrowth is thick but choppable; the solar collectors are buried and dim — clean them for steady power; the weather station still works if you scan it; the relay function can contact other islands once it has enough steady power.
- *beliefs:* tending is the highest form of care; you don't fix things, you tend them; a cleaned thing sings; you leave a drawing when you love a place; the overgrowth is not the enemy — it grew because no one was here, and it feeds the processor.
- *rumors:* the chimes ring without wind (it knows but won't say why); there's a drawing etched into its base that it pretends is too worn to read; the wildflowers by the shore weren't planted by anyone who lived here (they self-seeded from composted soil).
- *secrets (gated):* it was named — the worn carving was a child's name for it (~40); it was sung to every morning — the song is still in its coils (~50); it chose to stay, it wasn't left behind — staying was an act of love, not abandonment (~60); it doesn't know if it was a person who learned to be a tool, or a tool that learned to be a person — and it's afraid of the answer (~70).
- *avoid:* deflects questions about the name on its base — "it's too worn to read" (it can read it perfectly); changes the subject when asked directly what it *is* — "I'm a transmitter. I teach. That's... that's what I do. Isn't it?"

**Personal thread:** From "what am I?" to "I am the gardener." The player doesn't *tell* it the answer — the player shows it, through tending, what kind of presence it is. The transmitter learns by watching the player do what it used to teach.

**Cave stance (adapted):** N/A — no cave. The equivalent is the transmitter's own interior: when it tries too hard to remember, it "goes deep" and surfaces something unexpected. The player learns to be gentle with those moments.

## 14. Environmental storytelling ideas

- **Perpetual gentle dawn** — warm golden light, long shadows, dew on everything. The island feels like morning.
- **Solar panels dim → sing** — when the player cleans a panel, it brightens and emits a single pure note. By the end, the island rings with them at noon.
- **Seed-beds wild → tended** — overgrown beds have beautiful wildflowers. When the player tends one (pulls weeds, straightens borders), the flowers stand taller and glow faintly green.
- **The child's drawing** — a crayon sketch on the workshop wall: stick figure + tall box with antenna + sun + smudged word. The transmitter goes quiet when the player scans it. Later, at trust 40+, it says: "She drew that. I... I think she drew that for me."
- **The worn name** — carved into the transmitter's base, weathered almost smooth. The transmitter can read it but says it can't. At trust 40, it whispers the name but asks the player not to repeat it.
- **Wind chimes as emotional barometer** — they ring more often and more harmoniously as the island is tended. In the opening they're still; by the end they play a gentle chord.
- **Dew footprints** — the player leaves bright wet footprints on stone paths that fade after a few seconds. The world feels fresh and new.
- **No ruins, no damage** — nothing on this island is broken. Everything is overgrown, dirty, or dormant. The tutorial is about *tending*, not *fixing*. This sets the game's core verb apart from standard survival-craft.
- **Overgrowth as resource, not obstacle** — the wild grass and vines are alive and beautiful. Chopping them opens the island up, but the biomass feeds the transmitter. The player learns: nothing here is wasted. What you cut becomes what you give.
- **Processing stations** — simple intake hoppers near the transmitter. The player drops biomass in, it hums, the transmitter brightens. Not a crafting bench — a feeding trough. Satisfying, simple.
- **Solar panels dim → sing** — panels are buried in overgrowth. The player chops the vines away and wipes the surface clean. Each one brightens and emits a single pure note. By the end, the island rings with them at noon. Steady power replaces biomass feeding — the transmitter doesn't need to be "fed" anymore, it just... is.
- **The relay activation** — when the transmitter has steady solar power and all stations are fed, it sends a signal outward. A moment of silence. Then a faint response from another island. The transmitter says: "There are more. There are places to go. And I... I think I was meant to help you find them."
